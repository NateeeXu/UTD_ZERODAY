# Triage Loop: real-geography data

> **What is real and what is synthetic.** The *places* are real: H3 cells, census tracts and blocks, demographics, commuting patterns, schools, hospitals, fire stations, communication masts, coastline, terrain, earthquake shaking and liquefaction zones all come from public datasets (listed below). The *disaster events* and *every policyholder* are synthetic. The hurricane tracks are hypothetical. The earthquakes are USGS planning scenarios, not real events. Each household is a simulated record. Its attributes are drawn at random from real tract-level shares, so none of them describes a real person or a real policy. The same goes for SMS replies, free-text messages, claims and field reports: all of them are simulated.

Generated by `sim/real/` (see "Regenerate" below). The format is `EventBundle` from [`shared/types.ts`](../../shared/types.ts), including the v2 optional fields.

| Folder / file | Contents |
|---|---|

| `tx-corpus-christi/backtest-1..3.json` | Three other hypothetical hurricanes on the same real geography: Cat-2 landfall at Port Aransas, Cat-4 landfall on south Padre Island, and a slow Cat-1 rain event. 15,000 households each, with field reports for 12% of non-holdout households. |
| `ca-bay-area/live.json` | USGS **HayWired M7.05** Hayward Fault scenario ShakeMap over the Alameda County corridor (Berkeley/Oakland through Hayward to Fremont). 30,000 households. No `field_report`. |
| `ca-bay-area/backtest-1..3.json` | USGS ShakeMaps for **San Andreas (Peninsula) M7.38**, **Calaveras North+Central M7.0**, and **Hayward (South) M7.0**. 15,000 households each, with field reports. |
| `*/sim-meta.json` | Simulator diagnostics: where the deliberate quirks are, plus study-area notes and fallbacks. **Not part of the contract. The engine must not read it.** |

Household ids are prefixed by region and event (`tx-live-h000001`, `ca-backtest-2-h000123`), so they are unique across all bundles.

## What is approximated
- **Study area.** H3 res-8 cells that contain 2020 blocks of the county inside a bounding box, with ≥ 5 estimated households (block HU100 × tract ACS households/housing units).
  - Texas: bbox 27.53–27.93 N, 97.70–97.03 W (Corpus Christi, Calallen, Robstown, Flour Bluff, North Padre and Mustang Islands, Port Aransas). 449 cells.
  - California: Alameda County blocks within 9.5 km west / 3.5 km east of a hand-digitized, approximate Hayward Fault trace. The trace is used only to select cells, never for hazard. 611 cells.
- **Tract assignment.** `HexCell.tractGeoid` is the tract that contributes the most population to the cell, using real block-to-tract membership. It is not a nearest-point approximation. `Household.tractGeoid` is the tract of the block the household was placed at.
- **Household placement.** Households are allocated to cells in proportion to ACS-scaled population (largest remainder, at least 1 per cell). Inside a cell, each household goes to a 2020 block internal point chosen in proportion to block population, plus up to 120 m of jitter (kept inside the cell).
- **Coverage flags are assumptions.** `windCovered` 97%. Texas `floodPolicy` = 6% + 50%·exp(−coastKm/2.5) + 10% if ground < 3 m (about 18% overall). California `earthquakeEndorsement` **12%** (assumption, in line with the commonly cited ~10–15% California take-up; no policy data used). `phoneOnFile` 88%. `medicalDevice` = old-simulator rates (9% if 65+, 2.75% otherwise) × tract SVI disability rate / 13%.
- **`awayDaytime`.** P = clamp(LODES resident workers per household / 1.8, 0.03, 0.85), ×0.3 for 65+ households, ×0.85 with children (assumption: about 1.8 adults per household).
- **Event clock.** `World.eventLocalHour` and `eventWeekday` are derived from `landfallIso` in the region's time zone (America/Chicago, America/Los_Angeles) with `Intl`. The simulation uses exactly these values:
  - Work hours = weekday 08–18; school hours = weekday 08–15.
  - Work/school effects apply **only to earthquakes**. Hurricanes are forecast days ahead, so offices and schools are closed and people shelter at home.
  - Live CA (10:40 Friday) and CA backtest-2 (12:05 Thursday) are in work and school hours. CA backtest-1 (03:42) and backtest-3 (20:30) are not.
  - Texas events: 02:00 Sat (live, backtest-1), 22:00 Wed (backtest-2), 17:00 Thu (backtest-3).
- **Hurricane wind (hypothetical tracks).** Straight track through the landfall point. V = vmax(s)·g(d)·asym·exposure + N(0,3):
  - vmax decays over land with a Kaplan–DeMaria-style factor, exp(−0.095·hours over land). Over-land distance comes from the OSM coastline side test.
  - g = 1 inside Rmax, (Rmax/d)^0.6 outside.
  - The right side of the track is stronger: asym 0.85–1.0.
  - exposure = 0.82 + 0.18·exp(−coastKm/2.5).
- **Flood.** Still-water level = surgeFt · alongshore(d) · exp(−coastKm/L), with the peak about Rmax right of the track and the left side ×0.35. Depth = level − ground elevation (Terrarium, ft). On top of that come 6 rain pockets centred on real low-lying inland cells, scaled by lowness, plus N(0,0.25). Anything under 0.5 ft becomes 0. No hydrodynamic model (SLOSH/ADCIRC) is used.
- **Earthquake shaking** is the real ShakeMap MMI, which already includes site amplification (Vs30). Liquefaction is the CGS regulatory zone coverage. Zones mark areas that need investigation; they are not a probability.
- **Tower outage (synthetic, correlated with hazard).**
  - Hurricane: 85·sigmoid((wind−116)/8).
  - Earthquake: 80·sigmoid((MMI−9.2)/0.25).
  - Both add one strong cluster (amplitude 95, r 1.7 km) centred on a **real OSM mast**, plus 2–3 weaker clusters on other exposed masts.
  - The hurricane's primary mast is the one right of the track, 0.5–6 km from the coast, with the highest wind. The quake's is picked among the 5 masts with the highest score.

## Hazard index (engine-facing, the same definition for both hazards)
`hazardIndex = clamp(score / 3.2, 0, 1)`, where score is on the simulator's damage scale (1 ≈ minor, 2 ≈ severe, 3 ≈ uninhabitable):
- Hurricane: `score = max(w,f) + 0.3·min(w,f)`, with `w = max(0,(windMph−60)/26)` and `f = floodDepthFt/2.7`.
- Earthquake: `score = max(0,(MMI−6.8)/1.6) + 0.8·liquefaction·clamp((MMI−6.5)/1.5,0,1)`.

For earthquakes `windMph = 0` and `floodDepthFt = 0`. `TruthRow.peril` is `'earthquake'` (or `'none'`), and `claim.peril` is `'earthquake'`.

## Truth and signals
- **Damage.** Per-household severity, using the same thresholds as the synthetic simulator: < 1.0 → 0, < 1.8 → 1, < 2.6 → 2, otherwise 3.
  - Hurricane: as in the synthetic simulator, plus mobile home +0.9 to wind, older building +0.15, a hidden "vulnerable" 6% +0.7.
  - Earthquake: shake score of household MMI (cell MMI + N(0,0.3)), plus older building +0.35, mobile home +0.4, vulnerable 6% +0.7, liquefaction (household inside a zone with probability = cell coverage, MMI ≥ 7) +N(0.8,0.3), plus N(0,0.3).
- **needs_help.** logit = −4.6, plus:
  - 1.9 for damage 3, 0.8 for damage 2
  - 0.5 age65, +1.2 if age65 and alone, 0.5 age75
  - **0.7 disability**
  - medical device: 1.8 if outage > 60%, otherwise 0.5
  - **noVehicle**: 0.4 + 0.6 if flood > 4 ft (hurricane), 0.2 (quake)
  - 0.6 if flood > 4 ft
  - **−1.8 if awayDaytime during a daytime event** (empty home)
- **SMS** (English-only check-in, sent t = 60–180 min after landfall, or 20–120 min for quakes). Reply probability 0.78·(1−outage)^1.6, then:
  - ×0.7 age65, ×0.85 age75
  - ×0.6 damage 3, ×0.5 needs_help
  - **×0.55 limitedEnglish**
  - **×0.6 away during a daytime quake** (silence without danger)

  Delays, damage noise, the over-reporting district (+1 at 45%) and senior under-reporting (−1 at 30%) are the same as in the synthetic simulator. Away households report damage with ±1 noise half the time; they are guessing.
- **Free text.** For ~25% of replies (`text` + `language`), the text is built from template phrase banks in en/es/zh/vi/tl/ko. It is consistent with the structured `safety`/`damage` and mentions household needs (oxygen/CPAP/dialysis, elderly or bedbound, kids, no car, no power). Some replies are deliberately ambiguous: about 20% of damage-1/2 replies say "house is a mess" or "not sure, it's dark", and away households say "not home, don't know about the house".
  - Limited-English households always write in their language. Bilingual households do so 45% of the time (assumption). Languages without templates fall back to English.
  - The structured fields are the simulated ground truth of what the text means.
- **Claims.** Same as the synthetic simulator, ×0.15 for flood without `floodPolicy`, and **×0.08 for earthquake without `earthquakeEndorsement`**. With ~12% take-up, claims are a weak signal for quakes. That is a deliberate bias.
  - **Renters** insure only contents and additional living expenses; the structure is on the landlord's policy. They file ×0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 as often for damage 1 / 2 / 3. **Condo** (HO-6, interior only) files ×0.8 / 0.8 / 0.9 (assumptions).
  - High-renter areas (student neighbourhoods, apartments) are therefore quieter on the claims channel for the same structural damage. In this build the multipliers cut live claims by about 22% in both regions.
- **Family reunification (school-hours quakes).** A household has a reunification need when school is in session, it has `schoolAgeChildren > 0`, and its `nearestSchoolId` is in a cell with hazardIndex ≥ 0.5.
  - There is no truth field for this. Instead, these parents reply more often (×1.25, cap 0.95) and twice as fast when their phone works.
  - The count is in `sim-meta.json` (`reunificationHouseholds`) and in the `real:check` output.
- **Field reports.** Backtests only. 12% of non-holdout households, t = 1440–4320, from 20 adjusters. Damage equals truth; 6% of safety answers are `unsure`.

## Deliberate biases (what the engine has to cope with)
1. **Silence is not safety.** The strongest outage cluster sits on a real mast in the hardest-hit area. Needs-help and damage-3 households reply less often.
2. **Silence is also not danger.** Away-from-home households (LODES-based) reply less during a daytime quake, but they are rarely in danger.
7. **Renters are quiet in claims.** For the same structural damage they file far fewer claims (contents/ALE only).
3. **Limited-English households** reply less to the English-only SMS, and some of them reply in Spanish, Chinese, Vietnamese, Tagalog or Korean.
4. **Analyst under-marked a hard-hit cluster** (radius 2) and **over-marked a low-hazard cluster** (radius 2). Hurricane zones come from a forecast track shifted 1–2 km sideways. Quake zones come from a ShakeMap smoothed over a radius-2 neighbourhood that ignores liquefaction (zone 2 ≈ smoothed MMI ≥ 8.8, zone 1 ≈ ≥ 7.9).
5. **Over-reporting district** (radius 3): SMS damage gets +1 with 45% probability. **Seniors under-report** (−1 with 30% probability).
6. **Uninsured perils are quiet in claims**: flood without a flood policy, and especially earthquake without an endorsement.

Quirk locations are in `*/sim-meta.json`.

## Fallbacks used in this build
- IPEDS, ACS tenure and enrollment, and 2020 group quarters all downloaded successfully, so the OSM university fallback was not needed.
- The Census Data API now requires a key, so ACS comes from the **table-based summary file** instead. These are the same estimates, streamed and filtered to the two counties.
- The USGS elevation point service (EPQS) took about 20 s per point and the 3DEP ImageServer timed out, so **AWS Terrain Tiles** are used for elevation.
- There is no public Hayward Fault trace in the pipeline. A hand-digitized approximate trace is used **only to select the study corridor**.
- No other fallbacks: PL 94-171, ACS, LODES, SVI, NCES, OSM, ShakeMap and CGS all downloaded successfully. Any future fallback is listed in `world.dataSources` (lines starting with `FALLBACK:`) and in `sim-meta.json`.

## Regenerate
```bash
cd sim
npm run real:fetch          # downloads/caches raw data into data/raw/ (gitignored); takes ~10-20 min the first time (ACS files are national)
npm run real:tx             # data/real/tx-corpus-christi/*  (options: --households N --backtest-households N --only live)
npm run real:ca             # data/real/ca-bay-area/*
npm run real:check          # contract validation + summary + silent-severe cells with nearby facilities
npm run typecheck
```
Generation takes under a second per region and is deterministic (fixed seeds). 50,000 households per world takes about 0.3 s and about 31 MB.
